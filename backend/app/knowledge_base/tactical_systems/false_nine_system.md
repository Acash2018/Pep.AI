# False Nine System

A false nine system uses a withdrawn centre-forward who drops between the lines to drag centre-backs out of position, create central overloads, and open vertical channels for runners from deep. The team attacks through layered movement rather than a fixed reference point in the box.

Ideal traits for the false nine include scanning, link play, disguised passing, tight-space dribbling, and the intelligence to choose between dropping, spinning, and combining. Wide forwards and attacking midfielders must time runs into the vacated centre-back zones, while midfielders provide late arrivals into the box to replace the missing target.

Risk indicators include forwards who only operate on the last shoulder, wide players slow to attack vacated central space, midfielders without goal-scoring instinct from deep, and teams unable to defend the long-pass-over-the-top response that opponents often use against the structure.
