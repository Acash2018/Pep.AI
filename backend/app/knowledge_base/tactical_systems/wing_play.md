# Wing Play

Wing play stretches the opponent horizontally, attacks through wide overloads, and generates chances from crosses, cutbacks, and byline deliveries. The team prioritizes width, repeated wide entries, and arrival of bodies into the box from multiple angles.

Ideal traits include one-versus-one dribbling, crossing accuracy from varied positions, repeat-sprint capacity for wide players, late-arrival timing from midfielders, and aerial threat from forwards and far-post runners. Full-backs and wingers must coordinate overlap and underlap patterns while central players attack near-post, far-post, and cutback zones simultaneously.

Risk indicators include narrow wingers who avoid the byline, full-backs without final-third quality, forwards weak in the air or poor at attacking the cutback zone, and midfielders who fail to arrive in the box at speed.
